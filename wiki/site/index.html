Isometric photography for Minecraft servers

Izomap

Put a camera in your world, aim it with a live preview and take a photo of what it sees. Izomap casts a ray for every pixel, then prints the picture onto maps that hang on any wall: from a single map to a 16x9 mural, seen by every player, with no resource pack.

What it does

A live preview in your hand

The map in your off hand shows what the camera sees, updated with every click. Turn, tilt, zoom and move it until the frame is right, before a single photo is taken.

Isometric, or the horizon

An orthographic camera, like an isometric game: the classic 30 degree view of a whole town, or a low shot across the sea with a sky behind it.

Light, shadow and sky

Sun shadows, ambient occlusion and dark interiors. Dawn, day, dusk and night skies, with the sun, the moon in its phase and stars that stay put as the camera turns.

Depth of field

Keep one distance sharp and let the rest fall away: a miniature tilt-shift look on a tilted camera, a subject lifted off its background on a level one.

Real maps on real walls

Every photo is cut into 128x128 maps in item frames. They survive restarts, never change unless you retake them, and come down whole if a frame is broken.

Every cost is yours to set

Permissions decide how large and how expensive a photo may be, per group. Colors, filters, frames and every message are plain files you can change.

How it works

Place a camera

Right-click a block with the camera from /izocam item. It starts at the classic isometric angle.

Aim it

Click the camera to turn, tilt, zoom or move it, and watch the preview map in your off hand follow.

Take it, hang it

Pick a size from one map to 16x9, take the photo, and hang it on any wall with a ghost that shows where it will go.

Gallery

Every picture on this page is a photo Izomap took, at the size it took it.

Color filters

Seven filters ship with the plugin, and a server can write its own: chains of brightness, contrast, saturation, tint and more, applied before every color is matched to the map palette. They cost nothing per pixel.
